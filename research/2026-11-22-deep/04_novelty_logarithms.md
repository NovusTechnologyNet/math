# 04 Prior-art search: binary BBP-type formulas for logarithms of primes (log 23 and the classification below 100)

Date of search: 22 November 2026. WebSearch calls used: **26 of 29**. WebFetch, curl and pdftotext were used for primary pages.
Tags: [READ] = I opened the page or PDF text and saw it. [SNIPPET] = seen only in search-result text. Quotes are at most 15 words.

## Summary

1. No prior statement found in 26 searches that proves nonexistence of a binary BBP-type formula for log 23, or for any log p, in the generality of class F. No source found that states the classification below 100.
2. The problem is stated as open in the three places we were told about, and I re-read all three. [READ] Chamberland 2003, Bailey-Borwein-Plouffe 1997 (open problem 5), and Bailey's compendium (April 8, 2023 version: list "certainly not complete", all-primes question open).
3. The closest prior work is Borwein-Borwein-Galway 2004, Theorem 4 plus Remark (i). It uses the same primitive-prime-factor idea (Bang). It excludes only non-Aurifeuillian Machin-type formulas. It says it could not exclude Aurifeuillian ones. [READ]
4. Pomerance (acknowledged in BBP 1997, section 5) showed that 23 has no identity of the Mersenne-ratio type, because each Phi_n(2) has a new prime factor. This is a Mersenne-only precursor of our method. [READ]
5. Barsky, Munoz, Perez-Marco, "On the genesis of BBP formulas" (Acta Arith. 2021; arXiv v4 Feb 2023) says 23 is the first prime outside the group generated by 2 and the numbers 2^N - 1. This is a Mersenne-only statement and not a nonexistence theorem for BBP formulas. [READ]
6. No source found that uses Baker's theorem, linear forms in logarithms, or the class F of all rational-periodic series for this question. Nothing found about Gaussian norms of (1+i)^n - c combined with the order of 2 mod p as a criterion. [READ for the BBG/Chamberland/Bailey/Barsky/Adegoke texts, SNIPPET for the rest]
7. OEIS has A104885 (binary), A165132 (ternary: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13), A207040/A088962 (Chamberland's generalized Gaussian-Mersenne primes) and A057429/A182300. No sequence with terms 2,3,5,...,73 as a "complete list below 100", and no sequence beginning 23, 47, 53, 59, ...: the OEIS term search for 23,47,53,59,67,71,79,83,89,97 returned no match. [READ]
8. No MathOverflow or Math StackExchange thread found (3 targeted searches). Absence of evidence only.
9. No Bailey compendium version later than April 8, 2023 found. No post-2023 paper found on this question.
10. Most threatening item: BBG 2004 (Theorem 4 and Remark (i)). Our Theorem G closes exactly the gap they name, and for the wider class F, but conditionally on Baker.

## What Bailey's compendium says (exact, with version/date)

- Version read: "A Compendium of BBP-Type Formulas for Mathematical Constants", David H. Bailey, dated **April 8, 2023** on the title page, from https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf (WebFetch saved the PDF; text extracted with pdftotext). [READ]
- An earlier version dated 1 Mar 2004 exists (corpora copy) [SNIPPET only: the title line in search results]. I could not open it (host not resolvable) and so cannot compare wording.
- Section 2 "Logarithm formulas" [READ]:
  - log n has a binary BBP formula whenever n factors completely over primes whose logs have one (the series are combined).
  - Derivations given: log 3 from log 2; Harley's Aurifeuillian route via Re log((1+i)/(1-2^n)) and the factorization 2^(4n-2) + 1 = (2^(2n-1) + 2^n + 1)(2^(2n-1) - 2^n + 1); J. Borwein's observation that both sets derive from Re log(1 - (1+i)^k / 2^n)-type expressions (garbled in extraction, so wording paraphrased).
  - The list (9) of primes p with a binary BBP formula for log p: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 29, 31, 37, 41, 43, 61, 73, 109, 113, 127, 151, 241, 257, 337, 397, 683, 1321, 1613, 2113, 2731, 5419, 8191, 43691, 61681, 87211, 131071, 174763, 262657, 524287, 2796203, 15790321, 18837001, 22366891, 4278255361, 4562284561, 2932031007403, 4363953127297, 4432676798593.
  - Wording: the list is "certainly not complete", and it is unknown whether all primes have the property, or whether the list is finite or infinite (paraphrase; the PDF says "it is unknown whether or not all primes have" it).
  - No criterion is stated. 23, Chamberland, Pomerance, Baker and the Borwein-Borwein-Galway paper are not mentioned in that section (grep of the extracted text; the Borwein-Borwein-Galway paper may still appear in the reference list, not checked). Nothing on nonexistence.
  - The 2023 list omits 331, 1429, 14449, 26317, 38737, 65537, 246241, 268501, 279073, 312709 that OEIS A104885 contains (see below). Not a conflict with our results, but our "complete below 100" statement matches both lists.
- Non-binary: "One can also obtain BBP formulas in non-binary bases for logs of certain integers"; a base-10 example is given (formula 84). Ternary formulas for log 2,3,5,7,11,13 are on p. 24 per OEIS A165132 [SNIPPET for the page number]. No ternary criterion stated.

## Closest prior work (ranked, with the gap to our results)

1. **Borwein, Borwein, Galway, "Finding and excluding b-ary Machin-type individual digit formulae", Canad. J. Math. 56 (2004) 897-925.** [READ, full text via curl + pdftotext]
   - Theorem 4: no non-Aurifeuillian binary Machin-type BBP logarithm formula for ln 23 or ln 89. Proof: a Machin-type formula means 23^n = 2^t * product of (2^m - 1)^(n_m); 23 and 89 are both primitive prime factors of 2^11 - 1 (Bang's theorem), so 89 cannot cancel. Same argument for the pairs (47, 178481) and (29, 113).
   - Remark (i): "unable to exclude" a formula for ln 23 using Aurifeuillian generators; they judge it unlikely and say it would need "truly massive" generators. Remark (ii): ln 113 does have an Aurifeuillian formula, via 2^28 - 1 and 2^7 - 2^4 + 1 = 113.
   - Also gives an Aurifeuillian restriction: for odd p, ord_p of 1 - (1+i)2^(-m) = 0 forces a congruence on ord_p(2) mod 4 (extraction garbled; see paper).
   - Gap to ours: their class is Machin-type, i.e. Q-linear combinations of fixed generators ln(1 - 2^-m), Aurifeuillian generators. Our class F is all series with rational periodic rho, which strictly contains these. They do not use Baker's theorem (no occurrence found in the text) and do not decide the Aurifeuillian case. They give no classification of primes below 100. Their abstract says that, to their knowledge, constants with no Machin-type BBP formula have no BBP formula of any form known [SNIPPET].
2. **Bailey, Borwein, Plouffe, "On the rapid computation of various polylogarithmic constants", Math. Comp. 66 (1997) 903-913.** [READ, NASA copy]
   - Says it is not known whether log 23 is in their class SC*; notes 2^11 - 1 = 23 * 89 so either both log 23 and log 89 are in SC* or neither; thanks Pomerance for showing no identity of their type (5.2) exists for 23, "a consequence" of each cyclotomic value at 2 having a new distinct prime factor; lists "Is log(23) in SC* in base 2?" as open problem 5.
   - Gap: Mersenne-ratio identities only; no Gaussian norms; no Baker; no general class. Note the "either both or neither" remark is consistent with our Theorem G (they would be linked).
3. **Chamberland, "Binary BBP-Formulae for Logarithms and Generalized Gaussian-Mersenne Primes", J. Integer Seq. 6 (2003), Art. 03.3.7.** [READ] Zbl 1073.11076 [SNIPPET].
   - States it is still unknown whether log 23 has a binary BBP formula; cites Pomerance for the form (1) (Mersenne ratios). Lists products such as 23*89, 47*178481, 53*157, 59*3033169, 67*20857, 71*122921, 79*121369, 83*8831418697, 97*673 as on his list. Claims every odd prime p appears alone or in a product, since p divides 2^(p-1) - 1. Open question on which m give prime numerators of exp(2 Re log Phi_m((1+i)/2)).
   - Gap: constructive only. No nonexistence. Its list of "product" primes is a negative-evidence table that matches our excluded set: 23, 47, 53, 59, 67, 71, 79, 83, 89, 97 are exactly the primes below 100 that are not alone on his list (the "alone" primes below 100 are 2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,29,31,37,41,43,61,73 per his and Bailey's lists). This is a consistency check, not a proof.
4. **Barsky, Munoz, Perez-Marco, "On the genesis of BBP formulas", Acta Arith. (2021); arXiv:1906.09629v4 (13 Feb 2023), section 4.** [READ: arXiv PDF text]
   - Says 2 and 2^N - 1 generate a subgroup of the rationals for whose elements binary BBP formulas for log exist; "the first prime" outside it is 23; checks that 23 and 89 appear only together in 2^N - 1 and never in 2^N + 1 (via ord_23(2) = 11).
   - Gap: a statement about a subgroup generated by Mersenne numbers (no Gaussian or Aurifeuillian factors), not a proof that no BBP formula exists. It does not claim non-existence in general.
5. **Adegoke, Oliver Lafont, Layeni, "A class of digit extraction BBP-type formulas in general binary bases", Notes Number Theory Discrete Math. 17(4) (2011); arXiv:1603.07395.** [READ] Gives formulas for log of an infinite set of primes (sections 4.1, 4.5), cites Chamberland. No statement about 23 or nonexistence found (grep).
6. **Kristensen, Mathiasen, "BBP-type formulas - an elementary approach", arXiv:2208.07696 (J. Number Theory 2023).** [READ locally: no mention of log 23 or Aurifeuillian by grep] Construction only.
7. Ternary: **A165132** and Adegoke's ternary paper (arXiv:1603.04974) [SNIPPET for the latter]: only logs of 2,3,5,7,11,13 known; no criterion.
8. Pure number theory, not about BBP: Pomerance, "Cyclotomic primes", arXiv:2411.04213 [SNIPPET: primitive prime factors of Phi_m(2), Bang's theorem, conditional results]; Wagstaff, "The search for Aurifeuillian-like factorizations", Integers 12A (2012) [SNIPPET]. They give tools only; no group-generation statement or BBP link found.

## Known or conjectured criteria

- No explicit conjectured criterion for which primes have a binary BBP log was found in any source read. The only partial statements are:
  - (a) sufficient: p divides a product that can be cancelled using cyclotomic or Aurifeuillian factors (Chamberland; Bailey; Harley; Borwein).
  - (b) necessary for Mersenne-ratio and non-Aurifeuillian Machin-type formulas: p must not share a primitive-prime-factor "friend" in some 2^n - 1 (BBP 1997 via Pomerance; BBG Theorem 4). Example pairs given by BBG: (23, 89), (47, 178481), (29, 113), where 113 is rescued by an Aurifeuillian factor.
  - (c) BBG Remark (i): Aurifeuillian case open, thought unlikely for 23.
- Bailey's compendium states no criterion (2023). OEIS A104885 states none.
- Barsky et al. identify the subgroup generated by 2 and 2^N - 1 and name 23 as the first prime outside it, without a criterion for BBP.
- Queries on Zsigmondy/Bang/Aurifeuillian combined with BBP returned only BBG, Chamberland, Bailey and Adegoke papers. Nothing on Baker's theorem in this context was found (two queries).
- Base 3 and other bases: only A165132 (log 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13); no criterion found.
- Pure number theory (items 5 of the brief): searches for "multiplicative group generated by Mersenne numbers", "products of cyclotomic values Phi_n(2)", Gaussian Zsigmondy returned only background (Zsigmondy/Bang theorem; Pomerance's cyclotomic primes; Drauschke-Tasche on cyclotomic values in Z[i] as a citation only [SNIPPET]). No paper found that determines the group generated by the values Phi_n(2) and Aurifeuillian factors. Not opened: Drauschke-Tasche 1987 (Archiv der Mathematik) and Xie's 2024-26 work on large prime factors of cyclotomic values (seen only as named in a snippet).
