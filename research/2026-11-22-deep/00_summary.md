page and database reads (arXiv, OpenAlex, OEIS, GitHub, Mathlib, lean-lang.org, journal

## 1. Is our work already known?

Three separate passes found no earlier statement of the classification (dimensions 8 and
13), the complete list of zero relations, the "no formula avoids the slow terms" theorem, the
2/3 optimality bound, or the non-existence of a formula for log 23.

- 57 targeted searches (reports 03, 04).
- A citation sweep: about 700 records of papers citing the ten closest works, by title and
  abstract (report 08).
- A close reading of the ten closest papers (report 12).

Closest earlier work, and the gap:

| Work | What it has | What it does not have |
|---|---|---|
| Borwein, Borwein, Galway 2004 | The same primitive-prime method in Z[i]; excludes Machin-type formulas for pi in bases other than 2, and non-Aurifeuillian ones for log 23, log 89 | No classification in base 2, no Baker, no count of zero relations (asked as Research Problem 2), Aurifeuillian case left open |
| Barsky, Muñoz, Pérez-Marco 2021 | A way to generate null formulas; an efficiency measure (BBP 1, Bellard 7/10); notes that 23 is the first prime outside the group generated by 2 and the 2^N - 1 | No completeness, no lower bound; asks whether all degree-1 null formulas arise from their theorem |
| Bailey's compendium (2023) | Table of zero-relation counts found by experiment; list of primes with a formula for log p | Says the zero relations are not understood; says the list of primes is not complete and gives no criterion |
| Lafont 2011; Adegoke, Lafont, Layeni | Individual zero relations and families of formulas | No count, no exclusion |
| Takahashi 2020 | Two formulas for computing and checking pi together; calls Huvent's formula the one with fewest terms known | No proof that fewer is impossible |
| Broadhurst, unpublished manuscript (2000) | Unknown: Bailey cites it as the one discussion of zero relations | Not online; still the main unknown |
| A self-published preprint of August 2026 on the "annihilator lattice" of BBP formulas (removed by Zenodo on 30 October 2026 as out of scope; an archived copy of 8 September exists) | Starts, as we do, from Borwein, Borwein and Galway's closed form (their equation 64): zero relations as multiplicative relations among numbers 1 - 1/alpha; numerical tables for chosen pairs (b, n) | In the part read (abstract and first two sections): no classification of all binary formulas, no dimension 8 or 13, no optimality, nothing on logarithms; it says it does not answer Problem 10. The rest was not read |

No source says that a formula has the least possible number of terms, or that one is
impossible for log 23.

## 2. Published questions the theorems answer

Inside the class F (base 2^B with period B or 2B), and with Baker's theorem where stated:

| Question | Where it is asked | Our theorem |
|---|---|---|
| How many independent binary Machin-type zero relations exist? | Borwein, Borwein, Galway 2004, Appendix D, Problem (2) | Theorem A: exactly two (no Baker needed) |
| Does log 23 have a binary BBP formula? | Chamberland 2003, section 2; Bailey's compendium | Theorem G: not in F |
| Which primes have one? | Bailey's compendium (list "not complete") | Theorem H: below 100, exactly the fifteen listed |
| Why do zero relations occur for certain b and n? | Bailey's compendium, section 12 | Theorem C: all 27 binary rows of degree 1 are explained and counted |
| Is there a faster formula than Bellard's? | Bellard's page (1997) | Theorem E: 2/3 term per bit is the least possible; Huvent's formulas attain it |
| Do all degree-1 null formulas come from one construction? | Barsky, Muñoz, Pérez-Marco 2021, section 1 | Theorem C lists them all; the comparison with their construction is not done yet |

Not answered, and not to be claimed: Bailey, Borwein and Plouffe's 1997 question whether
log 23 is in the complexity class SC* (a different question); bases other than 2; binary
families with other ratios n/B (report 14 found no known formula there, and no theorem
excluding one, other than our Part IV for pi).

## 3. Baker's theorem: a public Lean proof seems to exist

- The Lean leaderboard's problem "Baker-Wüstholz" has four accepted solutions (first on
  21 September 2026). All sources are private. Its statement has integer coefficients and an
  explicit lower bound; it would not give the form we need (algebraic coefficients).
- A public branch `baker` of the GitHub fork `mkaratarakis/mathlib4` (the author of the Lean
  proof of Gelfond-Schneider) has 19 files, about 7,400 lines, with
  `Transcendental.baker`: exactly our hypothesis. Apache 2.0 header, no `sorry` in the main
  file. It is dated 23 and 24 September 2026, unmerged, and not announced anywhere the
  searches reached (report 13). It targets Lean 4.35.0-rc2; we use 4.36.0.
- It has not been downloaded, built or audited here. `lean/PiFormula/BakerBridge.lean` now
  proves that a theorem of that shape gives our `BakerTheorem`, so if the branch builds and
  its axioms are clean, every result of Part III becomes unconditional with one line.

## 4. The landscape in November 2026

- On 20 November 2026 a Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (17 million lines) was
  released; the Lean and Mathlib communities certified it the next day.
- The Lean leaderboard's hard list is being cleared by AI labs: most number theory items
  have several accepted solutions.
- Earlier in the autumn: Lean formalizations of Fermat's Last Theorem and of the
  classification of finite simple groups were announced by labs (report 06).
- There is a backlash: a declaration by Fields medalists, a call to boycott, and a batch of
  722 AI manuscripts of which some were withdrawn. The Erdős problems site froze its status
  labels in October.
- Consequence: formalizing a famous known theorem is no longer a way for one person to stand
  out, and "AI solved it" is now a negative signal to many mathematicians. What is still
  valued (report 06, report 15): a new statement that can be audited in minutes, a human who
  understands it and can answer questions, a specialist brought in early, and honest scope.

## 5. Where one person with an AI can still do something real

From reports 05, 09, 10 and 11, most promising first:

1. **Our own niche.** New theorems about BBP-type formulas: the stated open questions in
   Bailey's compendium and in Borwein, Borwein, Galway's Appendix D (arctan(2/7), a criterion
   for all primes, Gaussian-integer bases after Zudilin 2024, other bases). Lowest risk,
   and the only place where we already hold results nobody else has.
2. **The cost of a checked computation** (Takahashi 2020; y-cruncher has used Huvent's
   formula by default since 2024). See the result in section 7.
3. **Conjectured identities for pi.** The Ramanujan Machine continued fractions and Zhi-Wei
   Sun's conjectured series (more than 150 new ones in March 2026): outsiders have proved
   dozens by difference equations and the WZ method, and Lean can check the certificates.
4. **Three-term Machin formulas.** Finiteness is proved (Yamada); the full explicit list is
   open. Our Gaussian-integer method applies.
5. **Visible gaps in Mathlib's lists.** Bang's theorem (base 2) and Zsigmondy's theorem have
   no Lean entry; a pull request for Zsigmondy was closed in September 2026. There is no
   BBP, Bellard or Huvent formula in Mathlib. Our library has most of the pieces.
6. **Certified improvements of constants** in the Tao, Davis, Ivanisvili repository of
   optimization constants, and claimable tasks in the explicit analytic number theory and
   prime number theorem projects.

To avoid: irrationality measures and irrationality of new constants (a race between
professionals and labs in 2026), normality, and the famous prime problems.

## 6. How to be taken seriously

Checklist from Buzzard's post of 12 November 2026 and the Lean community's statement
(report 15): a statement file that imports only Mathlib and that a human has read; a check
that the Lean statement says what the paper says, with a non-vacuousness lemma; a pinned
toolchain; `#print axioms`; `leanchecker`; `lake comparator`; continuous integration; the
prompts and logs published; a specific statement of what the AI did.

Gates (reports 06, 11, 15): arXiv needs a personal endorsement for a first mathematics
submission and allows two submissions a month since October 2026. Mathlib requires AI use to
be disclosed and bans comments written by a language model; several AI-heavy pull requests
were closed this autumn. The Journal of Integer Sequences takes unaffiliated authors and
requires the words to be the author's own. Zenodo removes material it judges out of scope.

## 7. New result from this pass: the cheapest checked computation of pi

Takahashi computes pi with one formula and checks it with a second one that shares most of
its series, instead of running one formula twice. `verify_pair_optimum.py` puts this in our
framework. (Filled in below.)

Model. Scale two formulas for pi to the same value; their difference z is a zero relation.
A wrong value of one series changes the two results differently exactly where z is not zero.
A series on which the two formulas agree has to be computed twice (Takahashi does this with
his S(10,5) and S(30,15)). So the cost is |supp z| + 2 |supp rho minus supp z|, minimised
over zero relations z and formulas rho.

Computed in exact arithmetic over all of F, per 60 bits (not yet machine-checked):

| Scheme | Terms per 60 bits |
|---|---|
| Huvent's formula twice, at shifted positions | 80 |
| Huvent's two formulas used as a pair (base 2^12) | 65 (the least possible in base 2^12) |
| Takahashi's pair, by his own count | 64 |
| Least possible when no series is computed twice | 63 (seven pairs of supports) |
| Takahashi's pair with coinciding series merged | 60 |
| Least possible in F | 60, attained only by Takahashi's pair |

So Takahashi's pair is the best possible in F, which was not known, and it costs 60 rather
than 64: his S(12,3) and S(20,5) are the same series up to the factors 3 and 5, and so are
S(12,9) and S(20,15). Merging them means one modular exponentiation modulo 15(4k+1) instead
of two; whether that is free in an implementation has not been tested. The script first
rebuilds his two formulas in our coordinates as a test: both lie in the 8-dimensional space
and both have value pi.
