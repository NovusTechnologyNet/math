## 1. Barsky, Munoz, Perez-Marco, "On the genesis of BBP formulas"

Bibliographic: arXiv:1906.09629; Acta Arithmetica 198 (2021), 401-426. [READ] https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09629
(version list). Full text of v4 (13 Feb 2023) and v1 (23 Jun 2019) read from
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.09629 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.09629v1 (via local pdftotext). v2 (7 Jun 2020:
one reference added, old Sect. 5 moved to appendix) and v3 (22 Jul 2020: references added, "final version to
appear in Acta Arith.") were [NOT READ] (only the arXiv version table). v4 comment on arXiv: corrects minor
mistakes in the published version.

* Theorem 1.1 (Sect. 1, p. 4 of v4) [READ]. Statement (paraphrase of the garbled extraction): for s in C with
  |s-1| < 1 (or |s-1| = 1 and n >= 2), the sum over j >= 0 of I_n(s) terms
  (1-s)^(j+n) ... equals (s^(n-1)/(n-1)!) log s + B_n(s), where I_n are iterated integrals of 1/u, I_n(s) =
  A_n(s) log s + B_n(s), A_n, B_n in Q[s] with deg = n-1; equivalently
  log s = n s^(n-1) ... sum_{j>=0} (1-s)^(n+j)/(j+n) ... - s^(n-1) B_n(s) (formula typeset badly; exact
  coefficients NOT reliably extracted; consult the PDF). Taking s = 1 - 1/b gives BBP-like formulas for log s;
  complex s with real and imaginary parts gives formulas for pi.
* Null formulas, Sect. 1 (p. 4), v4 [READ]: "It is natural to ask if all null BBP formulas of degree 1 can be
  obtaining combining formulas from Theorem 1.1 for different values of s." (sic, same wording in v1, p. 3-4.)
  The preceding sentence says null formulas can appear when comparing formulas at different s, e.g. s = 1/2
  and s = (1+i)/2 give the null formula (4) = Proposition 5.3.
  Also: "The origin of null BBP formulas is somewhat mysterious" (Sect. 1, p. 2).
* Remark 1.2 (v4, p. 4-5) [READ]. Efficiency of a BBP-like formula (3) = m_* / log b, m_* the number of non-zero
  coefficients in A = (a_1..a_m); "this measures the number of non-zero summands for going to the next step";
  for b = 2 use log base 2. Values: BBP formula (1) has efficiency 1; Bellard (2) has 7/10, "a 43% faster".
  The text says nothing on a best known or best possible value; it only hopes the approach will help find "more
  efficient BBP-formulas for pi by more powerful algebraic computer search". Intro (p. 1-2) says Bellard's
  "seems to be the most efficient one for the purpose of computation of deep binary digits" of pi. Difference v1/v4:
  in v1 the same text sits unlabelled in the introduction (p. 4); in v4 it is labelled Remark 1.2 and the intro
  cross-refers to it. The measure and the values (1, 7/10, 43%) are identical in both. Their measure equals our
  "terms per bit" (standard measure) when the period is the length n and b = 2^B... (their m_*/log2 b with m_* the
  non-zero coefficients per block of length n: this matches ours only when n = 2B-type blocks; the match was not
  re-derived here, so treat the identification as "from memory, unverified" beyond the stated definition).
* log 23 and the group generated by 2 and 2^N - 1, Sect. 4 (p. 13, end, v4; the paragraph after Theorem 4.3) [READ]:
  "The numbers 2 and 2^N - 1, N >= 1, generate a multiplicative subgroup of Q"; for elements k of it there are
  binary BBP formulas for log k; "The first prime that it is not in this subgroup is k = 23." They show 23 and 89
  always appear together in 2^N - 1 (2^11 - 1 = 23 x 89) and never in 2^N + 1. They do NOT claim that log 23 has
  no binary BBP formula, and do not pose it as a question; the statement is only about the subgroup. (Same
  paragraph in v1, p. 14.) Formulas of this sort are credited to Chamberland [15] in v4.
* Huvent: no occurrence of "Huvent" in v1 or v4 text [READ, grep]. Bellard's formula is proved as Theorem 5.5
  (Sect. 6 / 5).
* Other open items: normality of pi (Introduction, general); "We plan to carry this out in the future"
  (new formulas). No explicit conjecture on completeness beyond the null-formula question above.

## 2. Borwein, Borwein, Galway, "Finding and excluding b-ary Machin-type individual digit formulae"

Bibliographic: Canad. J. Math. 56 (2004), no. 5, 897-925. DOI 10.4153/CJM-2004-041-2. [READ] (Cambridge PDF
via pdftotext)
https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/BB7919C8E1AE66878AE8D7BC7F0EBFA3/S0008414X0003385Xa.pdf/finding-and-excluding-b-ary-machin-type-individual-digit-formulae.pdf
(journal version; preprint not compared).

* Definition 1 (Sect. 2.4, p. 903) [READ]: for b >= 2 not a proper power, a real number has a Z-linear or Q-linear
  Machin-type BBP arctangent formula to base b iff it is a Z-/Q-linear combination of the generators
  arctan(+-b^(-m)); for b = 2 also "Aurifeuillian" generators arctan(1/(1 - 2^m)) = arg ln(1 - (1+i)2^(-m)).
  "A non-Aurifeuillian formula is one which does not use Aurifeuillian generators." The minimal set need not be
  linearly independent. Appendix B reduces any Machin-type formula to Bailey's form. Definition 3 (Sect. 3):
  logarithm analogue, with generators ln(1 - b^(-m)) and, for b = 2, Aurifeuillian ln generators (identity (44)).
* Theorem 4 (Sect. 3, p. 914) [READ]: "There is no non-Aurifeuillian binary Machin-type BBP logarithm formula for
  ln(23) nor for ln(89)." Proof by Bang's theorem (ord_23(2) = 11, 2^11 - 1 = 23 x 89). Attributed to Pomerance,
  "first mentioned briefly in [BBP97, Sect. 5]". Remarks: same argument excludes 47 and 53 (2^23 - 1 =
  47 x 178481) and 29 and 113 (2^28 - 1), non-Aurifeuillian case only.
* Theorem 7 (Sect. 4, p. 918) [READ]: for b > 2 not a proper power, there is no Q-linear b-ary Machin-type BBP
  arctangent formula for pi. (Abstract: pi has no such formula for b other than 2.) For b = 2 they give hand-found
  formulas (11)-(13) (Sect. 2.5, p. 904-905); the abstract notes pi does have such formulas when b = 2.
* Appendix D "Comments and Research Problems", p. 923-924 [READ], numbered (1)-(11):
  (1) 3+i = 2+(1+i) = 4-(1-i) gives two distinct binary formulas for arctan(1/3); "trivial zero relation or
  interesting?"
  (2) "How many Q-linearly-independent binary Machin-type BBP arctangent zero relations exist? Are there good
  upper bounds?"
  (3) Is there a simple way to derive the original BBP formula (63) from a Machin-type formula? (They say it does
  not appear to be a Q-linear combination of generators.)
  (4) Infinitely many bases with no b-ary formula: does it follow from Chebotarev?
  (5) Use Jacobi-symbol homomorphisms as extra exclusion tools. (6) Combine with PSLQ.
  (7) Zsigmondy for number fields (Schinzel, Bilu-Hanrot-Voutier) should give an effective version for Q[i] and an
  exclusion criterion for arctangent formulas analogous to Theorem 6.
  (8) "For the binary case, can we find exclusion criteria that deal with Aurifeuillian generators in logarithm
  formulae?"
  (9) Generalize the definition (b in Z[i], p, q in Z[i,k]) to treat arctan and log together.
  (10) Can the techniques decide whether pi is a linear combination of L(1, b^m, n, j) (general n, fixed b)?
  "probably require a good understanding of Q[zeta_n, b^(1/n)]".
  (11) "How can we justify the idea that our limited set of 'Machin-type' BBP generators gives all ... 'interesting' arctans and logs?"
* Abstract: "To the best of our knowledge, when there is no Machin-type BBP formula for a constant then no BBP
  formula of any form is known for that constant." Also Sect. 2.5 (p. 905): arctan(2/7) is the first arctangent
  for which they could not find a binary formula.
* Our file says "Problem (7) suggests Zsigmondy-type results in Q(i)" and "Problem (10) asks the general question":
  confirmed for (7); for (10) the text is as above (relation to a linear combination of L(1,b^m,n,j)).

## 4. Bailey, Borwein, Plouffe, "On the rapid computation of various polylogarithmic constants"

Bibliographic: Math. Comp. 66 (1997), no. 218, 903-913. [READ] preprint https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/digits.pdf
(pdftotext). The AMS site returned 403, so the published version was [NOT READ]; the preprint's section numbering
may differ from the journal.

* Sect. 7 "Questions" (p. 11) [READ], exactly the list: 1] decimal digits of pi in SC; 2] Show pi is in SC in all bases;
  3] Are e and sqrt 2 in SC in any base?; "4] Is log(2) in SC in base 10?"; "5] Is log(23) in SC in base 2?"
  Sentence before them: "Similarly the treatment of log is incomplete".
* Intro (p. 3): the identities "show that ... {log(2), ..., log(22)} are in SC. (We don't know however if
  log(23) is even in SC.)"
* Sect. 5: 2^11 - 1 = 23 x 89 "so either both log(23) and log(89) are in SC or neither is"; Pomerance showed an
  identity of type (5.2) does not exist for 23 (each cyclotomic value at 2 has a new prime factor).
* Sect. 2 (p. 5) after (2.15): "it is open and interesting as to whether G (Catalan) is itself in SC in base 2".
* Sect. 6 (p. 10-11): J. P. Buhler "has reported a proof that any identity for pi of the above form must have
  n = 2^K ..." (n in the form of the BBP-type identity with m, n; exponents garbled).
* GAP: "SC" in this paper is a complexity class (polylogarithmic space, polynomial time) for computing the d-th digit, not
  "has a BBP-type formula". A BBP-type formula puts a constant in SC, but the converse is not claimed. So
  Question 5 as literally worded asks about SC membership.

## 5. Chamberland, "Binary BBP-formulae for logarithms and generalized Gaussian-Mersenne primes"

Bibliographic: J. Integer Seq. 6 (2003), Article 03.3.7. [READ] https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL6/Chamberland/chamberland60.pdf
(pdftotext).

* Abstract/Intro: binary BBP formulas for log p for Gaussian-Mersenne primes and "generalized Gaussian-Mersenne
  primes" (via (1+i)^k / 2^n and cyclotomic polynomials), connected to Aurifeuillian identities (Sect. 3).
* Sect. 2, after the list of primes (p. 6) [READ]: "Carl Pomerance (see [2]) showed that 23 could not be written in
  the form (1); however, it is still unknown whether log 23 has a binary BBP-formula." Then: "Related questions are
  extensively dealt with by Borwein, Borwein and Galway[4]." Form (1) is a combination of logs of 2^n - 1 type
  generators (non-Aurifeuillian).
* Question stated in Sect. 2: "For which positive integers m is the numerator of the rational expression exp(2 Re
  log(m...(1+i)/2)) prime?" (generalised Gaussian-Mersenne primes; list of m < 3000). Not about completeness of null
  formulas.
* Theorem 3.1 (Sect. 3) [READ, statement not extracted]: Aurifeuillian-type factorization of the 4n-th
  cyclotomic value at 2; Example 3.2 (m = 8, n odd) is cited in our note for Phi_4n(2) = A_n A'_n.
* No conjecture on pi.

## 7. Kristensen and Mathiasen

Bibliographic: S. Kristensen, O. Mathiasen, "BBP-type formulas -- an elementary approach", arXiv:2208.07696 (v1, 16 Aug 2022);
published J. Number Theory (2023; reference from search result only). [READ] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07696 (v1).
The published version was [NOT READ].

* Abstract: a search method (LLL) and Sage implementation; a new BBP-type formula for pi sqrt 3; formulas in Pisot
  bases (e.g. pi in base 1 + sqrt 3).
* Intro (p. 2): after crediting Barsky et al., "A main feature of their work is that they find representations of zero as
  a BBP-type formula"; these null formulas "have been among the more mysterious ones in literature".
* Main Theorem 1 (Sect. 2): a point on a circle with an angle that is a rational multiple of 2 pi gives a BBP-type
  formula for an argument; the example gives pi/4 = BBP(1, 16, 8, A) with r = 1/2, a = 3, b = 8. A geometric (triangle)
  proof of Lafont's null formula (2) 0 = 2^12 BBP(1,2^12,24,CTB24(1/sqrt2,5)) - BBP(...,11)), Fig. 2 (Sect. 3).
  Table 1 (Sect. 4) counts known formulas by base and length.
* Sect. 5 "Concluding remarks and open problems" [READ]: (i) the abundance of zero formulas for the golden ratio base
  is "somewhat of a mystery"; they have "just not found all zero formulas for other numbers"; (ii) digit extraction in
  irrational bases is open; (iii) a geometric approach for higher degree is open. No question on completeness of
  integer-base binary relations beyond "(i)", which is about irrational bases.

## 8. Bilu and Luca, July 2026 preprint

Bibliographic: Yu. Bilu, F. Luca, "Multiplicative dependence in the sumset of multiplicative groups", arXiv:2607.28857 [math.NT],
v1, 30 Jul 2026 ("Preliminary version, comments are welcome"). [READ via summary] https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28857 and
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28857 (only the first 100,000 of 189,847 characters of the HTML were summarized;
the remainder was not read).

* Abstract (paraphrase): G, D finitely generated multiplicative subgroups of algebraic numbers with finite
  intersection ("almost disjoint"). Up to finitely many exceptions, two nonzero sums x1+y1, x2+y2 (x_i in G, y_i in D)
  are multiplicatively dependent only if x1/x2 = y1/y2 is a root of unity. For m >= 3 sums they discuss the possible forms;
  for m = 3, assuming the abc conjecture, a classification up to finitely many exceptions.
* Theorem 1.1 (m = 1): finitely many (x, y) with x + y a root of unity, effectively determinable. Theorem 1.2 (m = 2):
  non-effective in general (effective for rank 1 groups). Theorem 1.5 (m = 3, conditional on weak abc). Conjecture 1.6
  (general m).
* Relation to the numbers 1 - zeta*alpha: structurally similar (numbers t - r, r a root of unity, are sums of an element
  of <t> and an element of the roots of unity, groups almost disjoint, and our Step 2 classifies multiplicative
  dependencies among them), but the roots of unity are a torsion group of infinite order and our dependencies involve
  arbitrarily many (up to 24) of the numbers at once. Their main results are for a bounded number m of sums,
  non-effective, with finitely many exceptions, and the summary mentions no arctan, BBP or digit formulas. So
  "related in technique, not a source of our theorem". The exact applicability to 24 numbers was not checked.
