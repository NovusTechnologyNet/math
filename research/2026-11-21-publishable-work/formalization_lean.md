## 1. Is Zsigmondy's theorem or Bang's theorem in Mathlib (Lean 4) as of 2026? If not, is there a formalization elsewhere?

### Takeaway
Not in Mathlib: there is no `Mathlib/NumberTheory/Zsigmondy.lean` on master and Mathlib's own `1000.yaml` still lists Zsigmondy and Bang without a declaration; the one Mathlib PR (#28895, Harmonic) was closed unmerged on 18 Sep 2026. But a complete, sorry-free Lean 4 proof of Bang–Zsigmondy, including the exact statement "there is a prime p with orderOf (2 : ZMod p) = n iff n ≠ 1 and n ≠ 6", was merged into the Tau Ceti library (downstream of Mathlib, Apache-2.0 license) on 15 Nov 2026, and two older Lean versions (Lean 3, 2022; Lean 4 Harmonic PR, 2025) are still readable. I found no Isabelle AFP, Coq/Rocq, HOL Light or Mizar formalization.

### Inferences
- For our purposes Bang's theorem is available today in Lean 4 in exactly the form we need (existence of a prime with `orderOf (2 : ZMod p) = n` for n ≥ 2, n ≠ 6, plus the 2^n + 1 version). Two practical routes: depend on Tau Ceti (heavy, moves hourly with Mathlib master), or copy the seven files (71.5 kB, roughly 1,500 lines by my estimate from byte counts) under the Apache-2.0 license with attribution. The Harmonic PR file is an alternative source with Mathlib-style naming.
- The suggested Isabelle AFP entry "Zsigmondy's Theorem" does not appear to exist; the claim should not be repeated in a paper without a URL.
- Because the Mathlib PR was abandoned in September 2026 after the dependencies had merged, the helper API it needed (homogenization, `cyclotomic_six`, order lemmas) is in Mathlib, which lowers the cost of a self-contained Bang proof if we prefer not to depend on AI-authored code.
- Bang's theorem for a = 2 gives one primitive prime of 2^(4n) − 1, which divides one of the two Aurifeuillian factors of 4^n + 1; our lemma (3) needs a primitive prime in each factor, which is strictly stronger and is not covered by any formalization found.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Zsigmondy row of the cross-system 1000+ table (page truncation), so "no Coq/HOL Light/Mizar formalization" rests on absence of search hits and the AFP topic page, not on a positive check of each library.
- No dedicated search for "Aurifeuillian" formalizations or Schinzel's primitive-divisor theorem could be run after the search budget was exhausted; absence is supported only by `1000.yaml` (no title match), the Mathlib directory listing and the Tau Ceti roadmap.
- Why Harmonic deleted the PR's head repository (and whether a successor PR is planned) is not stated anywhere I read.
- Line counts for the Tau Ceti files other than `Zsigmondy.lean` and `Order.lean` are estimated from bytes, not counted.

## 2. What does Mathlib contain today for cyclotomic polynomials, orderOf in ZMod, GaussianInt, Real.arctan and Machin-like formulas, irrationality/transcendence of pi, Lindemann–Weierstrass, Gelfond–Schneider, Baker?

### Takeaway
Mathlib has nearly everything elementary we need: cyclotomic polynomials with integer-evaluation bounds and the order-of-a-root lemma, Fermat/Euler order lemmas in `ZMod`, lifting-the-exponent, `GaussianInt` as a Euclidean domain with the splitting criterion for rational primes, `Real.arctan_add` and four named Machin-like identities, and `irrational_pi`. It does not have the transcendence of pi, Lindemann–Weierstrass (PR #28013 open since Aug 2025), Gelfond–Schneider (PR #42911 open) or Baker's theorem; an effective linear-forms-in-logarithms bound for algebraic numbers is not formalized anywhere I could find.

### Inferences
- ℤ[i] as a UFD comes for free in Mathlib from `GaussianInt.instEuclideanDomain` (Euclidean domain, hence PID, hence unique factorization) — the instance chain is standard Mathlib but I did not open the instance declarations to confirm the exact names.
- Missing pieces we would write ourselves for ingredient (1): the factorization of a rational prime p ≡ 1 mod 4 into two non-associate conjugate Gaussian primes (Mathlib has the 3 mod 4 criterion and the sum-of-two-squares statement, not the full classification), the units of ℤ[i], and the bridge between arctan(1/n) and arg(n + i). `Complex.tan_arg`, `Complex.arg_mul_coe_angle` and `Complex.arg_pow_coe_angle` are the natural glue; alternatively the bridge can be done on the real side with `Real.arctan_add`.
- Ingredients (2), (4), (5) are covered by Mathlib plus the Tau Ceti files. Ingredient (3), the Aurifeuillian lemma, has no formal precedent: we would need to define L, M = 2^n ∓ 2^((n+1)/2) + 1, prove L·M = 4^n + 1 (a `ring`-level identity once exponents are handled) and then our own argument for a primitive prime in each factor.
- For the later Baker-dependent part: a qualitative Baker theorem over ℂ is claimed complete on a personal fork but unreviewed and unmerged; an effective bound for logarithms of algebraic (Gaussian) numbers, which is what a quantitative statement about arctangent forms needs, is not formalized anywhere found. The rational-logarithm result of `mjtb49` does not apply directly because arctan(1/n) is the argument of a non-real algebraic number. Plan on citing Baker-type bounds as hypotheses rather than formalizing them within weeks.

## 3. Formalizations related to pi: Machin formulas, BBP formula, Chudnovsky, irrationality of pi, transcendence; in which systems

### Takeaway
Individual Machin identities are formalized in Lean/Mathlib (four named lemmas) and in the Rocq standard library; the BBP series and digit extraction are formalized in Coq (Bertot–Rideau–Théry, Journal of Automated Reasoning); a Lean 4 Chudnovsky-formula project is catalogued in Lean Pool; irrationality of pi is in Mathlib; transcendence of pi exists in Isabelle, Rocq, HOL Light and (outside Mathlib) Lean. I found no formalization in any system of a classification or completeness statement for Machin-type formulas.

### Cited Findings
- Lean/Mathlib Machin-like lemmas: `Real.arctan_inv_2_add_arctan_inv_3`, `Real.two_mul_arctan_inv_2_sub_arctan_inv_7`, `Real.two_mul_arctan_inv_3_add_arctan_inv_7`, `Real.four_mul_arctan_inv_5_sub_arctan_inv_239` — [Mathlib docs: Arctan](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Trigonometric/Arctan.html)
- Rocq standard library `Stdlib.Reals.Machin` contains Machin-type identities such as `Machin_4_5_239` (π/4 = 4·atan(1/5) − atan(1/239)) (search summary of the library page) — [Rocq stdlib Machin](https://coq.inria.fr/doc/master/stdlib/Stdlib.Reals.Machin.html)
- "Distant decimals of π" (Yves Bertot, Laurence Rideau, Laurent Théry; arXiv Sept 2017, to appear in Journal of Automated Reasoning; older source): computes "1 million decimal digits of π and the billionth hexadecimal digit" in a formally verified way in Coq, using one spigot (BBP) formula and two arithmetic-geometric-mean methods; the BBP series is proved equal to π via Coquelicot; the digit-extraction lemma `NpiDigit_correct` is only a partial-correctness statement; the paper notes the Coq standard library "provides a formal description of Machin formulas" — [arXiv 1709.01743](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01743); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.01743)
- A search summary of the same paper says it notes that the HOL Light library contains a formalization of the BBP formula; my direct read of the first 100k characters did not find that sentence, so treat it as unconfirmed — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.01743)
- Chudnovsky: the Lean Pool archive lists a Lean 4 project "A Detailed Proof of the Chudnovsky Formula" following Lorenz Milla's arXiv:1809.00533, contributor `ldct` (search summary; my fetch of the paper saw Milla's paper only in the bibliography) — [Lean Pool paper, arXiv 2609.25199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25199)
- Irrationality of pi in Lean: `irrational_pi` — [Mathlib docs: Pi/Irrational](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Real/Pi/Irrational.html)
- Wiedijk's "Formalizing 100 Theorems" page (last update shown 2026-11-01; 100% of the list formalized; Lean 100, HOL Light 99, Isabelle 95, Rocq 80, Metamath 75, Mizar 71): #53 "Pi is Transcendental" — Lean (Trevor Morris and Yuyang Zhao), HOL Light (Daniel J. Bernstein), Isabelle (Manuel Eberl), Rocq (Sophie Bernard & Laurence Rideau); #56 Hermite–Lindemann — Lean (Yuyang Zhao), HOL Light (Daniel J. Bernstein), Isabelle (Manuel Eberl), Rocq (Sophie Bernard); #26 Leibniz's series for pi — Lean (Benjamin Davidson), HOL Light (John Harrison), Isabelle, Metamath, Mizar, PVS, Rocq — [Wiedijk 100 theorems](https://www.cs.ru.nl/~freek/100/)
- Lean declaration for Leibniz's series: `Real.tendsto_sum_pi_div_four` (Mathlib) — [Lean 100 theorems page](https://leanprover-community.github.io/100.html)
- Isabelle AFP: "The Transcendence of π" (Manuel Eberl, 2018), "The Hermite–Lindemann–Weierstraß Transcendence Theorem" (Eberl, 2021), "Gaussian Integers" (Eberl, 2020: Euclidean ring, "full classification of their primes", executable factorization, sums of two squares) — [AFP number theory topic](https://www.isa-afp.org/topics/mathematics/number-theory/); [AFP Gaussian_Integers](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/Gaussian_Integers.html)

- A 2026 repository formalizes an ineffective irrationality-type lower bound for linear forms in logarithms of rationals and says it grew out of "OpenAI's improvements to the irrationality measure of π", importing OpenAI's formalization through `jdb19937`'s repository — [GitHub repository](https://github.com/mjtb49/Bounds-on-linear-forms-in-logarithms)

### Inferences
- A machine-checked classification of all rational relations among π and arctan(1/2^m), arctan(1/(2^m − 1)) would be new as a formalization in every system: existing work proves individual identities, never the absence of others.
- Isabelle's AFP `Gaussian_Integers` entry is the only library found with a full classification of Gaussian primes; in Lean that step is ours to write.
- The OpenAI irrationality-measure formalization mentioned in the `mjtb49` README is relevant to the wider pi project and worth a separate look by whoever covers irrationality measures.

## 4. Tools that would speed up the work in 2026, and reports of people formalizing number theory with LLM help

### Takeaway
By late 2026 agentic LLM workflows are the normal way to formalize: Claude Code with the `lean4-skills` plugin and the `lean-lsp-mcp` server (which wraps LeanSearch, Loogle, Lean Finder and premise search) is documented in a published number-theory formalization; Anthropic reports dozens of Claude agents formalizing all of Fermat's Last Theorem in 11 days (Sept 2026) and three Claude Max plans formalizing Vinogradov's three-primes theorem in three days. AlphaProof remains application-only; Aristotle is commercially available; Gauss is in limited beta. The cautions are consistent: kernel-checked is not the same as correctly stated, and AI-written code does not meet Mathlib's review bar without expert supervision.

### Cited Findings
Search and editor tooling
- `lean-lsp-mcp` (MIT license, beta): MCP server exposing Lean LSP tools (`lean_goal`, `lean_diagnostic_messages`, `lean_multi_attempt`, `lean_run_code`, `lean_local_search`) and external search tools `lean_leansearch` (leansearch.net), `lean_loogle` (loogle.lean-lang.org), `lean_leanfinder` (Lean Finder), `lean_state_search` (premise-search.com), `lean_hammer_premise` (leanpremise.net), rate limited to 3 requests per 30 seconds; installed in Claude Code with `claude mcp add lean-lsp uvx lean-lsp-mcp` — [lean-lsp-mcp README](https://github.com/project-numina/lean-lsp-mcp)

## 5. Size estimates: how long comparable formalizations took

### Takeaway
Human-era comparables for a single classical number-theory theorem are 1–2k lines and months of part-time work (Zsigmondy in Lean 3: 1,211 lines; Ramanujan–Nagell: 1,811 lines); library-building projects ran to years (FLT for regular primes: about 10k lines, more than 200 Mathlib PRs). ## 6. Venues for formalization papers and their expectations

### Takeaway
ITP (LIPIcs, 16-page regular or 6-page short papers, formal sources mandatory) and CPP (ACM, 12 pages) are the standard conferences; the CPP 2027 deadline (10 Sep 2026) has passed and ITP 2027 has no call yet. Annals of Formalized Mathematics explicitly excludes formalizations of known results without new insight but is a good fit for a formalization that accompanies new mathematics. None of these venues requires Mathlib integration; Mathlib itself is backlogged and strict about AI-written code. Formalized Mathematics is Mizar-only.

## 7. Examples of formalizations of NEW (previously unpublished) theorems used to certify correctness, and how referees treated them

### Takeaway
Formalizing a new result at or before submission is now common, from PFR (2023, three weeks) to 2026 papers whose main theorems were produced and Lean-verified by AI systems. What the sources agree on is that the formal proof removes doubt about the argument but shifts scrutiny to whether the formal statement matches the paper's claim. I found almost nothing documented about how referees actually handled such submissions.

### Cited Findings
- PFR / Marton's conjecture (Gowers, Green, Manners, Tao; arXiv 9 Nov 2023): Tao reports the formalization "did pick up one very minor error" in the write-up and raised his confidence that the exponent 12 was rigorously justified "to 100%"; the project finished on 5 Dec 2023 — [Tao's blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2023/11/18/formalizing-the-proof-of-pfr-in-lean4-using-blueprint-a-short-tour/); [arXiv 2311.05762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05762)
- Bloom's density theorem on unit fractions (arXiv Dec 2021): the Lean project by Thomas Bloom and Bhavik Mehta formalizes "the main result of the preprint"; the arXiv record carries the comment "appendix co-written by Bhavik Mehta" — [unit-fractions project page](https://b-mehta.github.io/unit-fractions/); [arXiv 2112.03726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03726)
- Liquid Tensor Experiment: Scholze posed the challenge (post dated 5 Dec 2020) for the main theorem of liquid vector spaces; verified in Lean on 14 July 2022 with "continuous mathematical support from Scholze" — [Lean community blog](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/lte-final/)
- "Almost all primes are partially regular" (Evan Chen and 20 others including Ken Ono; arXiv 4 Feb 2026): a new theorem "produced automatically by AxiomProver from a natural-language statement of the conjecture" and "fully formalized in Lean/Mathlib"; no journal listed — [arXiv 2602.05090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05090)
- DeepMind's agent: 9 of 353 open Erdős problems and 44 of 492 OEIS conjectures resolved with Lean proofs — [arXiv 2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)
- Aristotle-verified new results (search summaries of the PDFs; not confirmed on the abstract pages): a counterexample to a conjecture of Sárközy "used Aristotle to obtain formal proofs of the four principal mathematical statements" (Lean v4.28.0, pinned Mathlib commit); a paper on gaps in multiplicative Sidon sets says a theorem was "autonomously discovered and formally verified in Lean by Aristotle" — [arXiv 2603.29992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29992); [arXiv 2605.02064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02064)
- Referee-oriented packaging (search summary of the PDF; the abstract page does not mention it): "Optimal strategies in the all-heads coin game" (Pfaffelhuber, 2026) includes a table mapping each numbered result to its Lean declaration and says a referee "who wants to mechanically verify the formalization without auditing the full proof modules can do so using the Lean comparator" — [arXiv 2604.22991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22991)
- Formalization as error-finder on a recent result: the Lean formalisation of Hölzl–Kleine–Stephan's strong n-conjecture bounds "found errors, omissions, and optimisation opportunities in the informal proof" — [arXiv 2610.16323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16323)
- Trust caveats stated by the producers themselves: the FLT repository says machine-checking "cannot confirm that each intermediate theorem means what its name says"; the `mjtb49` README tells readers to "confirm that `Challenge.lean` matches the mathematical claim" — [anthropics/fermats-last-theorem](https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem); [mjtb49 repository](https://github.com/mjtb49/Bounds-on-linear-forms-in-logarithms)
- Venue rules that bear on refereeing: AFM requires the paper to "make clear how informal statements match their formal counterparts, and flag and explain any potential mismatches" and expects reviewers "to consult the code alongside the paper"; ITP requires anonymised formal sources with every submission — [AFM aims and scope](https://afm.episciences.org/page/aims-and-scope); [AFM instructions for authors](https://afm.episciences.org/page/instructions-for-authors); [ITP 2026 call for papers](https://itp-conference-2026.github.io/cfp)
- Buzzard's expectation (Sept 2026): autoformalization "will ultimately make the review process for mathematics papers far less painful" — [Xena project blog](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/09/04/flt-anthropic-has-beaten-me-to-it/)
- A workflow paper in optimization counts a result as "formally verified" only after Lean's kernel check, an independent comparator check, and human confirmation that the Lean statements match the paper's claims (search summary) — [arXiv 2608.07407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07407)

### Inferences
- The emerging convention for a new theorem is: (a) a public repository pinned to a Lean and Mathlib version; (b) one short file containing only the final statements and the definitions they depend on; (c) `#print axioms` output and a comparator run; (d) a table in the paper mapping each numbered result to its Lean declaration; (e) an explicit AI-use disclosure. Doing all five costs little and answers the questions a referee can reasonably ask.
- For a classification theorem like ours the statement is the delicate part: "every rational linear relation ... is a combination of three" must be formalized as a statement about the ℚ-span (or kernel) of an explicit infinite family, and a referee will want to see that the Lean statement quantifies over all m ≥ 1 and all rational coefficient vectors of finite support. The definition file should be written and reviewed by a human even if proofs are AI-generated.
- A formal proof will not substitute for a readable paper proof at a mathematics journal; in every example found the formalization accompanies the human-readable argument.

- The published journal status of the PFR paper and of Bloom's paper was not confirmed from the arXiv records (both show no journal reference on the pages fetched).
